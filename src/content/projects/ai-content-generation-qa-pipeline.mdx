## Context

Growing a knowledge base from 250 to 2,056 articles by hand isn't a staffing problem you solve by writing faster — it's a pipeline problem. Every gap the detection pipeline surfaces still needs a draft, a fact-check, and a human sign-off before it's safe to publish to customers.

## How it works

The pipeline drafts new or updated knowledge base articles from source material and existing conventions, fact-checks the draft's claims against verified sources, and runs it through a QA validation pass before routing it into a review queue. Nothing publishes without a human decision point — the automation compresses the time between "we found a gap" and "a reviewable draft exists," not the accountability for what goes live.

## Outcomes

This pipeline is the engine behind the knowledge base's 8x expansion from 250 to 2,056 structured, AI-ready articles: it has generated 1,687 AI-assisted drafts and published 1,320 knowledge base improvements to date. Of the current knowledge base, approximately 97% was written or re-engineered through this process into documentation a conversational AI agent can actually reason over — not just search.

<LessonsLearned>
The bottleneck in scaling a knowledge base was never writing speed — it was review throughput. Building fact-checking and QA into the pipeline itself, rather than treating it as a separate manual step, is what made 1,320 published improvements possible without lowering the bar for what reaches a customer.
</LessonsLearned>
