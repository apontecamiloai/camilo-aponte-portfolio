In June 2025, I inherited a mandate, not a system: no knowledge base worth the name, no AI governance model, no connectors, no operational framework — just a green light to build one from scratch for a fast-growing SaaS company's support function.

The first thing I found was Fin, our AI support agent, running through one deterministic workflow with dozens of hardcoded branches. Every edge case meant another branch — it couldn't scale, and it couldn't reason. Instead of adding branch #47, I redesigned the architecture around one idea: **an AI agent should reason over evidence, not get routed through if/else logic pretending to be intelligence.** That shift became the foundation for everything that followed.

A year later, here's where it stands — told through the numbers, because the numbers are the part that's verifiable.

## Knowledge engineering: an 8x expansion, not just more pages

The knowledge base grew from roughly 250 loosely organized articles to 2,056 structured, AI-ready ones — an 8x expansion. Approximately 97% of that knowledge base was written or re-engineered by me into documentation an AI agent can reason over, not just search. That distinction matters: a human reader can skim past ambiguity; a retrieval system either finds one unambiguous answer or it doesn't.

Behind that growth: a gap-detection pipeline that identified 331 distinct knowledge gaps from real conversations and generated 1,687 AI-assisted drafts, 1,320 of which were published after human review. A parallel governance system removed 446 duplicate articles and audited 649 Guidance issues, so scale didn't come at the cost of quality.

## Reasoning, not routing

The layer I'm proudest of: 78 Guidance Prompts and 17 Procedures, built on live Data Connectors and structured retrieval, so Fin resolves issues on evidence instead of scripts. Resolution climbed from roughly 30% to nearly 70% within the first two months of that redesign.

## The plateau, and the mistake I almost made

Then resolution rate stalled. For about nine months, it held in the high-40s to low-50s despite the knowledge base continuing to grow. The obvious response — the one I nearly reached for — was to write more articles: more coverage, more edge cases documented, more nuance captured.

That would have been the wrong fix, because the knowledge base wasn't the bottleneck anymore. Looking at the conversations Fin actually failed on, the pattern wasn't missing information — it was questions that depend on live state: is this job still running, has this workflow failed, has the customer exhausted their quota, is this a known Engineering issue, does this account already have an available fix. No article, however well-written, can answer "is my job running right now." Only a live connector can.

## Operational AI: connecting the agent to reality

With Engineering, I led the integration of 19 production Data Connectors — spanning services including Billing, Publication Media, Authority Building, Report Builder, and Linear — across 12 internal services and 11 engineering teams, so Fin can see live product and account state instead of only reading static documentation about it.

Once the diagnosis changed, a second, later phase of connector work began: standardizing connector development across 8 product teams, with 6 connectors live at time of writing — distinct from the original 19-connector program, not an extension of its totals. A Known Issues detection layer started matching customer problems against existing Engineering tickets instead of re-diagnosing from scratch — on one measured day, about 29% of escalations already matched a known issue.

The reasoning architecture around all of this was redesigned too: Modular Guidance in place of one large prompt, Specialized Procedures for well-defined troubleshooting flows, a connector-first lookup order, evidence-first decision-making, and consistent escalation logic.

## Breaking the ceiling

The combination — not any single piece of it — is what broke the plateau. I want to be honest about what that means and doesn't mean: resolution rate is a system-level metric — it moves with Engineering fixes, Product changes, Billing issues, and Support process, not with AI work alone. What I can claim is the diagnosis and the fix: recognizing that the bottleneck had shifted from content to live operational context, and building the connectors and reasoning layer that closed it. Fourteen months in, average CSAT sits at 95%.

## Browser automation

Some of what a support agent needs to know only exists in the live product UI, not in any database — navigation paths, screenshots, current workflows. Playwright-based, authenticated automation that reuses logged-in sessions inspects product interfaces and flags documentation that has drifted out of sync with the UI, catching stale screenshots and outdated navigation paths automatically.

## Nine systems, zero precedent

Using Claude Code as my AI engineering copilot, I designed, built, and shipped nine production systems that didn't exist before this function did:

- **Knowledge Gap Detection Pipeline** — semantic retrieval that flags missing or outdated knowledge base topics automatically.
- **AI Content Generation & QA Pipeline** — drafts, fact-checks, QA-validates, and routes new articles through review before publishing.
- **Custom Answers Center** — builds ready-to-use Fin AI Custom Answer packages from real unresolved conversations. 40 packages generated to date.
- **Conversation Recovery System** — catches unresolved, unescalated, or falsely-resolved conversations and abandoned chats, then triggers proactive follow-up. 744 recovery attempts, 258 successful outcomes.
- **Customer Health & Churn Signals Bot** — flags at-risk accounts with evidence, before they churn.
- **Documentation Governance Auditors** — resolves duplicate and stale knowledge base content continuously as it scales.
- **Article Feature Tagger** — classifies and tags knowledge base articles by product feature.
- **KB Request Intake Bot** — classifies and routes incoming knowledge base requests from Slack and ClickUp automatically.
- **Weekly Fin/KB Reporting Bot** — generates the recurring performance report on schedule.

## The lesson

What I'm proudest of isn't any single piece of this. It's that knowledge engineering, live operational data, browser automation, and AI reasoning now work as one system — one that keeps getting better at helping customers, on its own, because every part of it feeds the others: gap detection feeds content generation, governance keeps that content trustworthy, and live connectors give the reasoning layer something real to reason about. When a metric plateaus, the instinct to add more of what worked before is exactly the instinct to check against the evidence first — no amount of better writing would have found this bottleneck. Only looking at where the agent was actually failing did.

## What comes next

I'm currently going deeper on LangChain, LangGraph, advanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation, and vector databases (Qdrant, Pinecone, Weaviate) — the next layer needed to keep pushing resolution rate and knowledge quality further, and to formalize evaluation across a system this size.
